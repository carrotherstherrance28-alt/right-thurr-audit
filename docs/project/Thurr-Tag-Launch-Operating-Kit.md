## 1. Launch Spine

The strategy is simple:

Person first. Mission always. Product as the token.

People follow the operator. They understand Thurr as the mission. They join the Founders List because the tag gives that mission a physical form.

Core line:

> The tag on your bag that introduces you.

Product line:

> Thurr Tag is a premium leather NFC bag tag for people in motion. It lives on your bag, opens your profile when tapped, and points every connection in the right direction.

Production gate:

> We move to production after 200 waitlist signups, 5 paid preorders, or 1 corporate gifting commitment.

Hardened interpretation:

- 200 waitlist signups means qualified signups with source tracking, not random low-intent traffic.
- 5 paid preorders means real paid orders after the sample direction is credible. If only this gate is hit, keep the first production order conservative.
- 1 corporate gifting commitment means signed purchase order, paid deposit, or written commitment from a real buyer. A warm DM does not trigger production.

## 2. Non-Negotiables

- Do not place a 150-unit production order until the gate is hit.
- Order samples first. Samples are the only upfront spend before demand is proven.
- Base Founders Edition is numbered, not personalized.
- Personalized Founders Edition is a separate made-to-order upsell with a longer ship window.
- NFC v1 uses a static short URL on the chip. The destination changes on the web side.
- Do not build custom NFC software for v1. Use Carrd, Webflow, Blinq, Popl, or a tracked redirect first.
- Do not lead with lost luggage recovery. That is a quiet utility feature, not the headline.
- Sticker language is real brand fuel, but stickers do not lead the premium launch. Include one Founders sticker in the box, sample sticker packs now, and open the paid pack only after tag demand is warm.
- Gift only to leverage: clients, referral partners, travel-heavy founders, and corporate buyers.
- Keep the first product tight: orange and black sample lanes, premium leather, privacy flap, debossed mark, NFC tap-to-connect. Lock one hero production color after samples unless demand clearly supports a two-color run.

## 20. Red-Team Holes To Fix Before Scaling

This is the skeptical pass. Claude is not connected in this workspace, so this section captures the outside-red-team structure and the separate Codex red-team findings. If Thurr Tag fails, it is likely because one of these issues was ignored.

1. The gate can false-positive demand. A signup, preorder, and corporate commitment are not equal signals. Corporate commitment must mean signed PO, paid deposit, or written budget approval.
2. The $75 price is exposed if the buyer compares it to basic tags or trackers. Thurr Tag must sell identity, access, and gifting value, not utility alone.
3. The category is easy to cheapen. A luggage tag sounds like a low-price accessory unless the page, sample, packaging, and founder story make it feel like access.
4. The buyer story can get blurry. Travel, NFC, gifting, lead generation, stickers, personalization, and four modes are too much for one cold visitor. V1 public copy needs one lead story.
5. There is no real product proof yet. CSS mockups validate visual direction, but premium buyers need sample photos, leather closeups, NFC demo video, packaging, and scale-in-hand.
6. Corporate gifting is underdefined unless the scope is tight. "Reporting" means tap count, page visits, lead form submits, and one manual recap PDF. Anything beyond that is a higher tier.
7. The color story must be consistent. The visual board can show orange and black, but production should lock one hero color after samples unless demand clearly supports both.
8. NFC privacy and SMS language need care. Lost-bag contact, tap reporting, phone capture, and corporate reporting create privacy expectations. Do not text anyone who has not clearly opted in.
9. Stickers can help or hurt the premium read. They are strong brand spread, but if they lead the launch, the product can feel like merch instead of a premium access object.
10. Outreach volume must rise. A beautiful page will not hit the gate without daily posts, direct DMs, follow-ups, and buyer calls.
